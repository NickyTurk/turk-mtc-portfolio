Practice

Educational Environment

Building a welcoming and productive environment for the students is one of the most urgent causes for any teacher. I strive to make my classroom fun and engaging through a number of aesthetic and interactive design choices. Aesthetically, I try to decorate in ways that represent my persona as a teacher. I had a cadre of skeletons that hung around my room last year, and going into my second year of teaching, I have been considering themes based on magic or maybe origami. Either way, the most important thing is presenting students with an environment that feels lively and inviting.

Instructional Practices

My teaching style has been affectionately described as "like the tasmanian devil became a math teacher." I tend to be high-energy, but it isn't possible for one person to match a classful of fifth graders. As such, I've created class procedures and practices that manage the students' energy and put it into something productive. Students enter the class ready to learn, sitting down and getting straight into their bellwork silently. Once class begins in earnest, I'm careful to ensure that all student work is performed and recorded properly. Students need rigid goals and routines to ensure that they succeed, and that is what I strive to provide them with.

Instructional Activity

I always push for high rigor in my students' learning while still trying to meet them where they are and scaffold them up to where they need to be. It's a difficult balance to strike, but one that can be struck with careful precision and strong design choices. My assignments are often made personally, and I work hard to ensure that they provide the needed supports without being too simple. Rigor is a vital aspect of mathematics, and the sooner my students understand that, the better off they will be in the future of their mathematical practice.
